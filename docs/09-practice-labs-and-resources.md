# 9. Practice Labs and Resources

Many of these have free tiers. Availability and pricing change, so check each site.

## Hands-on practice
- **TryHackMe**: guided rooms, including SOC and blue-team paths
- **LetsDefend**: simulated SOC alert queue
- **Blue Team Labs Online**: investigation challenges
- **CyberDefenders**: blue-team challenges and datasets
- **Elastic free trial / open-source Elastic Stack**: build your own SIEM lab
- **Wireshark** and **Sysmon**: free and essential
- **Your own home lab**: a couple of VMs (Windows + Linux) with VirtualBox or similar

## Official frameworks and references (free)
- **MITRE ATT&CK**: attacker behavior catalogue
- **NIST SP 800-61**: incident handling guide (Preparation, Detection & Analysis, Containment/Eradication/Recovery, Post-incident)
- **Lockheed Martin Cyber Kill Chain**
- **David Bianco's "Pyramid of Pain"**
- **CISA** advisories and alerts
- **Elastic documentation**: tutorials for Kibana, KQL and detection rules
- **Microsoft Sysinternals / Sysmon docs**

## Certifications (optional, not required to start)
Common entry points people choose: CompTIA Security+, CompTIA CySA+, ISC2 CC, Microsoft SC-200, and vendor-specific training for your EDR/SIEM. Pick one after you've done some labs, not before.

## Books and reading
Look for well-reviewed blue-team and SOC books through your library or official publishers. Always obtain them legitimately.

## Communities
- Local security meetups and BSides events
- Security subreddits and Discord communities (stay safe, don't share employer data)
- Follow analysts and researchers who explain things simply

## Staying safe while learning
- Practice only on your own systems or platforms that explicitly allow it
- Handle malware samples only inside an isolated lab VM, never on your daily machine
- Never run scans or tests on other people's systems without written permission
